# Hyponoia Human Feedback Guide

Human feedback is the primary learning target. The Internal Critic is an auxiliary diagnostic signal; users should not try to imitate or predict its scores.

## Recommended listening order

1. Listen without reading the Critic report.
2. Decide whether the result works according to the intended D-level.
3. Rate the six criteria from personal aesthetic judgement.
4. Add a controlled text instruction only when a specific change is wanted.
5. Read the Critic report afterwards as a comparison, not as the answer.

## Rating criteria

| Criterion | What to consider |
| --- | --- |
| `musicality` | Does the result feel intentionally organised, phrased, and developed? Musicality does not require conventional melody, tonality, or rhythm. |
| `coherence` | Do events belong to the same compositional world and create intelligible storytelling? |
| `richness` | Is there meaningful spectral, timbral, and morphological development? Richness is not simply more samples, louder sound, or more layers. |
| `transitions` | Do sounds emerge, connect, and leave naturally? Consider abrupt foreground and high-frequency events in particular. |
| `bloom_quality` | Does the composition open and develop organically through time, especially around its central formal growth? |
| `overall` | What is the user's holistic aesthetic response to this render? |

## Score anchors

| Score | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `0–39` | Does not work |
| `40–59` | Needs substantial improvement |
| `60–74` | Acceptable, but asks for correction |
| `75–84` | Good; no corrective increase is required |
| `85–100` | Excellent |

The corrective target is `75`. A criterion below 75 gently increases the control seeking that quality. A score at or above 75 does not request a corrective increase. Human-minus-Critic differences are preserved only as diagnostic evidence.

## D1, D3, and D5

Use the same quality criteria for all D-levels. The levels differ primarily in activity, density, layering, and formal intensity—not in the standard of musicality or coherence.

- D1 may be sparse and still receive high richness when a small amount of material evolves meaningfully.
- D5 may be dense and still receive low coherence when many events fail to form a narrative.
- Do not reward a render merely for containing more sounds.

## Controlled comment examples

Comments apply only to the level of the render being rated. An explicit prefix
may target another level, for example `D3: more energetic`. Use explicit global
language only when all three profiles should change.

- `smoother transitions`
- `more energetic`
- `fewer samples`
- `develop the selected sounds`
- `more coherent palette`
- `use different materials`
- `greater exploration`
- `less repetition`
- `more synthesizers`

Material development and cross-render exploration are separate intentions. `Fewer samples` or `develop the selected sounds` requests a smaller evolving palette inside one composition. `Use different materials` requests more palette rotation between successive renders.

## Examples

> The render is sparse, but its few sounds evolve organically: richness 80.

> It contains many interesting sounds, but they do not form a coherent story: coherence 55.

> The material is appropriate, but bright foreground events enter and leave abruptly: transitions 50.
